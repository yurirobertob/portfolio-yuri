UX / UI Case Study

Shiftsly: Designing a Mobile-First Scheduling Tool to Replace WhatsApp

Role: Founder & product designer — IA, onboarding, scheduling UI, billing flow Surfaces: Onboarding, weekly schedule view, pricing, Stripe checkout, in-product feedback Status: Live in production, onboarding real users since Feb 2026

The design problem

Cleaning-team managers in the UK were running their entire scheduling operation through WhatsApp threads: no structure, no visibility into who's covering what, and the buyer checks it standing in a client's driveway between jobs, not sitting at a desk. Existing tools like ZenMaid or Jobber are built for larger teams and don't fit how a 5-to-20-person crew actually works. The UI had to be simpler than a general scheduling SaaS and had to work on a phone first, since that's the only device the buyer actually has open during the day.

Key UI decisions

Onboarding Schedule UI Pricing Checkout UX Feedback loop

The schedule as a mobile carousel, not a desktop grid: the core object, a weekly schedule of teams, employees, shifts, and per-employee status, is the one screen managers open constantly. I designed it as a swipeable weekly carousel instead of a dense desktop-style grid, because the primary device is a phone held in one hand, not a monitor. The information architecture (teams > employees > weekly shifts > status) maps directly onto how a manager actually thinks about the operation, not onto a generic calendar-app data model.

Auth designed to remove a step, not add one: the sign-in flow uses Google OAuth or a magic link instead of a password. For a non-technical buyer signing up on a phone, that's one less field to fill in and one less "forgot password" support ticket, a small UI decision with a direct effect on activation.

Pricing designed around the unit the buyer actually thinks in: the three-tier pricing page is priced and labeled by headcount (number of cleaners), not by a generic per-seat model borrowed from a different market. That decision came directly from talking to the buyer persona, a cleaning-team manager thinks in "how many people," not "how many seats."

Billing & checkout UX

Shiftsly runs live subscription billing on Stripe across all three tiers, and I designed the full commercial surface, not just the product screens:

  • Plan comparison page that leads with headcount, not feature-checklists, matching how the buyer actually decides.
  • Stripe Checkout integration designed to keep the buyer inside the product's visual language up to the handoff point, so the transition to Stripe's hosted checkout doesn't feel like leaving the product.
  • Free tier as a real usable product (up to 5 cleaners), not a crippled demo, so the UI had to hold up fully at zero revenue and still make the upgrade prompt feel earned, not forced.

Closing the loop with real users: I built a dedicated in-product usability-test flow, a short multi-step form with NPS scoring, reviewed in an internal admin panel, so real friction points from real users feed directly into what gets redesigned next instead of relying on my own guesses about what's confusing.

What this demonstrates

End-to-end product design ownership

IA, onboarding, core UI, pricing, and checkout designed as one coherent system by one person, not handed off in pieces.

Real Stripe billing UX

A live three-tier subscription flow, priced around the buyer's actual mental model, not a generic SaaS template.

Designing for the real device and context

Every core decision, from the schedule's carousel layout to passwordless auth, traces back to how and where the buyer actually uses the product.

Feedback-driven iteration

An NPS-scored in-product test flow that turns real user friction into the next design decision, instead of opinion-driven redesigns.
