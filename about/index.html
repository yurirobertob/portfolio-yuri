About

Art direction, then software

Background

How I got here

I spent most of my career as an art director and graphic designer, on brand and campaign work for drinks brands and infrastructure clients. That is where the craft came from: type, composition, and knowing when something is finished.

In 2022 I moved to London for a year and it changed what I noticed. I stopped seeing visual briefs and started seeing broken workflows. The clearest one was cleaning team managers running entire operations out of WhatsApp threads, with no tool built for teams that size. That observation became Shiftsly.

What followed was not a folder of concepts. Three products went live, with authentication, payment providers, databases, scheduled jobs, and eventually users who noticed when something broke.

How I work

Design and build sit in the same head

Most design portfolios end at the handoff. Mine does not, because on every project here there was nobody to hand off to. I made the interface decisions and the schema decisions, and those two constrain each other constantly.

That has a specific consequence, and it is the most useful thing about working with me. I do not design things that cannot be built, and I do not discover the cost of a decision after someone else has committed to it. When I propose a flow I already know roughly what it costs to implement, where the state has to live, and what happens when the network call fails.

It also means I have been on the wrong end of my own decisions in production, which teaches faster than any critique.
